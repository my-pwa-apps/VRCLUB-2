# Principal experience and engineering review - 2026-10-04

Reviewed source revision: `8a5ab33`. Review mode: documentation changes only.
This is an evidence-bounded assessment, not headset, accessibility or security certification.
Findings and acceptance criteria are maintained in [BACKLOG.md](../BACKLOG.md).

## 1. Executive summary

VR Club is a client-side Babylon.js 9.28.0 nightclub with desktop exploration, WebXR,
a composed audio-reactive lighting show, procedural player embodiment and optional
WebSocket/WebRTC social presence. Its engineering is substantially stronger than a
typical rendering demo: ordered builds, explicit resource ownership, local assets,
real-model rig tests, safety controls and emulator-driven interaction tests exist.

The result is a convincing **stylized industrial club/music experience**, not a
production-certified ultrahyperrealistic venue. Lighting composition is its strongest
presence tool. Human behaviour, contact response, physically consistent illumination
and the lack of a real Quest frame-time baseline are the largest remaining gaps.

New evidence identifies three high-priority renderer issues: a separate ungoverned
moving-head flash source, master dimming that does not reach all fixture output, and
uptime-dependent sheet jumps during speed ramps. The dependency audit also currently
blocks the configured deployment gate. No new exploitable application vulnerability
was reported by the security source review.

Desktop is suitable for continued development and controlled demonstrations, but not
an unrestricted release sign-off. VR behaviour is extensively testable without a headset;
stable stereo rendering, comfort and sustained Quest 3S delivery remain unestablished.
Asset provenance/redistribution clearance remains an external public-release blocker.

## 8. Immersion assessment

Presence comes from tracked locomotion, spatial music, darkness, a composed show and
an embodied local player. The largest simulation reminders are fixed-role crowds,
nonresponsive near-field props, approximate grounding and inconsistent fixture state.
The screenshot supports a stage-centred club atmosphere, not a complete high-end venue.

Existing entrance/bar hierarchy, social micro-behaviour, physical contact and wear
items should remain; they are product-quality opportunities, not justification for
installing a full physics engine or increasing crowd count before headset measurement.

## 9. VR/WebXR assessment

Strengths: one readiness-gated entry point; desktop fallback; independent controller
registration; mutually exclusive teleport/movement swaps; default comfort mode;
snap turning; tracked eye-height preservation; opt-in haptics; world-space body/hand IK;
camera-owned XR effects and detached expensive desktop effects.

Limits: head-locked quick menu, one-active-pointer behaviour, synthetic hand orientation
testing, retained seated calibration on desktop return, unmeasured 4x MSAA cost and no
thermal/stereo comfort evidence. Hand tracking/near interaction are intentionally disabled.
Controller models are deliberately omitted to keep runtime asset loading same-origin.

Tested emulator identity is Quest 3, not proof of Quest 3S hardware delivery.
Do not generalize controller component mappings to every WebXR device.

## 10. Desktop assessment

WASD/arrow navigation, drag-look, presets, fixture controls and stream/file playback form
a useful non-VR experience. It is more than a debug camera, but flying/free-height
navigation and noninteractive venue objects still differ from a embodied club visit.
The native-button Space defect is a concrete accessibility regression; dim output and
post-XR restoration drift are existing visual inconsistencies.

Typing `w` in the stream URL field did not populate the camera's movement key list in
the browser probe; do not invent a typing-causes-walking finding.

## 11. Lighting and atmosphere

NOCTURNE's looks, bar-boundary structural decisions, palette ownership, kick punch and
gobo/laser exclusivity are substantial strengths. Preserve them while correcting the
final-output contracts. Safety practicals should be deliberately exempt from blackout.

Fix the separate flash source and phase/dimmer defects before adding clustered lighting.
A clustered prototype is an optional existing experiment, not an immediate release remedy.
Desktop lit-air particles must not be enabled in XR without headset evidence.

## 12. Materials and rendering

Shared materials, mutable fixture presets, unfrozen lit materials and opaque imported
models avoid known Babylon ownership/readiness/light-buffer pitfalls. Maintain bounded
light budgets and permanent light-slot membership. Accent locality alone is insufficient:
the actual compiled equipment slot must be asserted.

No current light owns a shadow generator. The backlog's claim that desktop generators
ground equipment was corrected; character contact blobs remain useful but cannot
replace directional contact response from changing fixtures.

## 13. Crowd and character realism

Character consolidation, shared material identity, clip pruning, tiered counts and one
contact-shadow submission are credible efficiency improvements. Procedural player
embodiment is not equivalent to autonomous believable NPC behaviour.

Fixed dance/conversation roles, limited reactive gaze/circulation and stylized faces
are the principal realism constraints. Extend controlled behaviours and animation
variation under measured budgets rather than adding more synchronized people.
Keep the real-Babylon/GLB rig tests; simple skeleton stubs cannot validate planted feet.

## 14. Spatial audio and music

The PA has real directional HRTF paths, corrected handedness, a listener following the
active camera, sub-bass routing, generated reverb and authored distance/zone filtering.
The browser suite tests actual left/right audio output, not only graph topology.

The model is designed acoustics, not wall-by-wall propagation: a distance function
and entrance threshold stand in for obstruction and room transitions. The crowd bed
is filtered noise from one centre emitter, not a recorded/socially distributed crowd.
That can be appropriate under music, but its realism has not been listener-tested.

Entry music is disclosed and can be disabled before ENTER. Podcast/feed lookup,
on-demand queueing, CORS requirements, finite-source resume and user-selected streams
have meaningful tests. Fix volume/diagnostics and calibrated onset analysis before
adding more audio effect complexity. Web Audio gain/compression cannot certify safe
headphone SPL.

## 15. Physics and interaction

Collisions and teleport blockers contain the player; controls share club-owned actions
across DOM and in-world surfaces. Venue objects generally are not grabbable or deformable,
and there is no rigid-body simulation to review for mass/friction correctness.

The best next physical-presence scope is the existing bounded contact-response item:
one rail/booth contact interaction and one responsive control, with tracked alignment,
visual confirmation and opt-in haptics. Do not promise a fully functioning bar.

## 18. Architecture and refactoring

Ordered classic scripts and the layered club class are pragmatic and strongly tested.
The inheritance chain still shares a large mutable state surface, and render-state
ownership is not uniformly expressed as final fixture-output contracts.

Prefer focused phase/state helpers and once-per-transition adapters over a wholesale
engine rewrite. Integrate phase and final dimming consistently; keep structural show
decisions on the musical grid. New pipelines must respect initial desktop setup, XR
detachment, restoration and explicit disposal.

## 19. Cleanup and dead assets

Production model copying is reference-derived, not a wholesale source-tree deploy.
The build's source-map handling deliberately omits embedded source content.
Do not delete Mixamo/source assets merely because provenance is incomplete: licensing
remediation and functional dance replacement require explicit decisions.

No blanket dead-asset deletion is recommended from filename searches. The most valuable
