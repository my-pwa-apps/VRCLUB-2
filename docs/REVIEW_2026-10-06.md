## 1. Executive summary

NOCTURNE is a substantial browser-based industrial club experience: composed lighting,
spatial music, people, player embodiment, a stocked bar, entrance and balcony. Its
strongest work is the integration of the show, resource-conscious batching and tested
XR/desktop paths. It is considerably more coherent than a collection of effect demos.

It is **not ready for an unrestricted production-quality/ultrahyperrealistic claim**.
The current review confirms:

1. Lint blocks the normal deployment pipeline.
2. Valid near-floor XR tracking can cause a 1.35 m artificial camera-height correction.
3. Invisible legacy walking barriers do not match the visible entrance area; the
   expanded venue still has incomplete teleport blockers.
4. A warmed offline visit loses all people, the local body and the reflection environment.
5. Sustained physical Quest frame timing and comfort remain unmeasured.

The greatest realism limits are human behaviour and contact, approximate light/shadow
transport, and motion/geometry contradictions, not insufficient polygons or texture size.
The best next investment is fixing those contradictions and measuring the real headset,
not introducing another expensive rendering pipeline.

- **H3:** from `(6,1.7,-4)`, a collidable ray toward -z hits invisible

  this review rechecked the missing blocker registration, not that controller arc.

## 8. Immersion assessment

Presence benefits from meaningful destinations, a player body, spatial PA, world-locked
VR controls and dark intervals between authored looks. The stage and bar were both
visually inspected; this is not a screenshot-only judgement of the code.

The most significant breaks are incompatible movement/geometry, low-pose corrections,
people who remain in looping roles, absent contact response and offline loss of people.
The bar is no longer an empty wall. There is no justification for rebuilding it merely
because an older review described it as absent.

Real stereoscopic presence, depth comfort and head-motion response remain unverified.

## 9. VR/WebXR assessment

Positive: explicit feature-detected session entry, loading gate, controller lifecycle,
teleport/snap-turn comfort default, separate XR post-processing, preserved tracked eye
height in ordinary cases, world-locked menu, bounded artificial steps and optional haptics.

Recent staircase work is implemented and has prior IWER evidence. It should not be
described as still needing camera gravity: the surface follower intentionally replaces it.
The private `_updatePosition` hook is an upgrade dependency that needs a regression test.

H2 is a separate tracked-height boundary failure, not evidence the entire stair fix fails.
H3 needs both walking and teleport tests. Include room-scale movement, seated/standing,
recenter, tracking loss and repeated XR entry/exit in hardware acceptance.

No hand-tracking, alternative-runtime or two-physical-eye compatibility claim is justified.

## 10. Desktop assessment

The product has real desktop UX: splash, mouse/keyboard navigation, audio and VJ panels,
camera destinations, graphics tiers and diagnostics. The unsupported-XR button is
disabled rather than pretending the integrated browser has a headset.

Online demonstrations are viable. Public release is not signed off: deployment currently
fails lint, navigation has a ghost wall, offline completeness is broken, the expanded
credits obstruct a control, and desktop rendering still drifts after XR.

## 11. Lighting and atmosphere

Keep the show director's single-owner/bar-boundary structure, safe-mode overrides, shared
flash governor and fixed light enablement. Tests exercise blackout, flash limits, sheet
motion and cue ownership. Passing timing tests is not medical photosensitivity certification.

The newer laser ribbons, analytic shell endpoints and instanced mirror spots are sensible
complexity reductions. Shell-only intersections do not model every prop or doorway;
check visible endpoints around the balcony/booth rather than reintroducing full-scene
raycasts by default. The intentionally removed laser-sheet floor line should stay removed.

Do not brighten the whole room to compensate for missing local response. VR base ambient
is now 0.015 with a 0.15 bounce cap; its actual perceptual balance requires the user's headset.
No shadow generator exists; NPC contact shadows help, but equipment/dynamic shadowing
remain approximations. A bounded contact solution is preferable to unmeasured extra lights.

## 12. Materials and rendering

Strengths: packed ORM, normal-map conventions, imported-material light budgets, opaque
model rules, no per-frame material freeze churn, one LED wall, and deliberate desktop/XR
pipeline separation. The 2048px graffiti atlas adds one merged alpha-tested paint mesh and
reuses wall relief; it is not a 4K-texture violation.

Retain the matte floor and explicit reflection fallback instead of lowering SSR thresholds
until concrete becomes a mirror. Fix desktop gain and restoration before subjective
material retuning: otherwise the comparison changes with mode history.

Glass thickness/refraction, human skin/eyes/hair and physically occluded indirect lighting
are fidelity gaps, not proof that more shader complexity is currently affordable.

## 13. Crowd and character realism

The online high-tier browser had 16 NPCs and a local rig. Balanced resource tests count ten
enabled NPCs with ten contact-shadow instances, at most six meshes per character.
Characters share source resources, vary phase/speed and occupy authored role slots.

There is a limited proximity yaw response and distance-based animation pausing; it would
be inaccurate to claim there is absolutely no response or LOD. However, fixed talking,
phone and watcher clips are not a social behaviour scheduler. The bartender talks but
does not perform a service task. Humans remain stylized and mixed in visual language.

Prioritize sparse gaze/state transitions and better role timing using the same skeleton
budget. Remote rig limits and seated grounding need validation before promising social VR.

## 14. Spatial audio and music

The real graph includes HRTF PA panners, consistent coordinate conversion, a filtered sub
path, air absorption, synthetic convolution tail, early reflection and controllable ambience.
The audio browser test exercises actual left/right energy after movement and head rotation.

The room acoustic model is distance/zone-based. Its occlusion threshold uses the legacy
z=-5 walk band, not actual walls/door visibility; it does not simulate balcony or furniture
occlusion. This is a realism limitation to calibrate with navigation, not a reason to add
unbounded raycasts or heavy convolution per object.

FFT=256 at 48 kHz groups roughly 0-2.2 kHz into "bass"; vocals/snare can affect show energy.
Rebanding without recalibrating the show would trade one defect for another. Default music
and recovery have unit coverage; live podcast availability was not tested here.

No digital compressor guarantees a safe sound-pressure level at a user's ears.

## 15. Physics and interaction

The interaction model is intentionally limited: distant controls, locomotion, camera
destinations, audio/social actions and opt-in haptics. Rails, bottles and decks are not
grabbable rigid bodies. This is acceptable scope if affordances remain clear.

Fix physical contradictions first (H2/H3), then prototype one meaningful contact response,
such as booth/rail hand feedback, within a measured budget. A full physics engine or
throwable bar inventory is not the next release requirement.

### VR

IWER is mono software rendering; it cannot establish stable 72/90/120 Hz, two-eye overdraw,
thermal headroom or motion-to-photon latency. The test also has no absolute XR-submission
ceiling. Do not choose one by copying a desktop number.

Current mirror reflection code no longer performs the historical full-scene per-ray picks.
Its old ~12 ms measurement and the old ~80-draw claim are not current measurements.
The batching item is closed on implementation/test evidence; the measured CPU target remains open.

## 18. Architecture and refactoring

The layered class chain and global script contract are understandable and heavily tested,
but cross-layer state and private Babylon APIs remain maintenance risks. H1 illustrates
the missing connection between runtime globals and lint configuration.

Prefer small shared-layout/ownership fixes over replacing the engine, reorganizing every
class or adding a second configuration layer. Restore a single source of truth for desktop
render settings and for navigation surfaces. Keep the existing loader/factory boundaries.

Teardown explicitly releases factories, containers, pipelines, XR helper, audio and UI
resources. That is a strong foundation; loading/disposal races still need targeted tests,
particularly when closing the direct-avatar-loading bypass.

## 21. Accessibility and comfort

The splash warning, pre-entry Safe Mode, comfort default, optional haptics, reduced-motion
desktop bob preference, separate ambience/music controls and native credits disclosure are
valuable. Safe Mode is intentionally opt-in; this review does not silently change that decision.

H2 is the most urgent newly validated comfort issue. Expanded credits remain an access
problem. No screen-reader, 200% zoom, contrast-across-all-cues or medical safety certification
was performed. Smooth walking and artificial falling still need in-headset evaluation.

## 22. Compatibility, security and privacy

Current positive evidence is Chromium plus IWER, not every WebXR device. Keep XR feature
detection and do not equate a browser displaying the page with immersive-session support.
The LAN HTTP instructions should not be treated as proof of a secure WebXR context:
verify the actual headset/browser setup, HTTPS or a supported loopback development route.

Proportionate security evidence: URL/DOM contracts and relay tests pass; networking and
microphones are opt-in; shared music requires a separate listen action. The splash discloses
default podcast-host contact. No live microphone/relay or adversarial network exercise ran.

Root advisories are zero; worker development tooling has four high affected nodes.
Undici advisory ranges include versions before 7.29.1, and Sharp's
[GHSA-wq5f-xc86-pv6w](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-wq5f-xc86-pv6w) covers <0.35.5.
Do not conflate inherited package severity with demonstrated application exploitability.
CI currently audits only the root lockfile. Asset clearance is separate from software security.
