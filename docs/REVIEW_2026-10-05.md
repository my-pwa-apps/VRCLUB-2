# Principal experience and engineering review - 2026-10-05

Source revision: `2671b79`. Review mode: documentation only, no runtime fixes or commits.
This reassessment distinguishes current evidence from the extensive historical
[backlog](../BACKLOG.md) and [previous review](REVIEW_2026-10-04.md).
It is not hardware, accessibility, legal or penetration-test certification.

## 1. Executive summary

VR Club is a substantial client-side Babylon.js nightclub, not merely a shader demo.
The composed lighting show, spatial PA, player rig, stocked bar, entrance and steel
balcony provide an increasingly coherent **stylized industrial music venue**. The
ordered production build, bounded light budgets, shared assets and real-engine tests
are strong foundations.

It does **not yet meet an unrestricted production-quality, ultrahyperrealistic claim**.
The largest remaining realism limits are human appearance/behaviour, limited physical
contact, approximate illumination/reflections and missing representative headset timing.
New runtime evidence also shows that comfort teleport can leave the newly enclosed
entrance, and persistent offline loading omits the people and reflection environment.

Desktop supports controlled online demonstrations. Public release still needs the
remaining asset-clearance decision and functional follow-up. VR interaction works in
the emulator, but enclosure integrity and sustained real Quest delivery are not ready
for sign-off. Fixing these paths and validating the actual hardware is more valuable
than adding another expensive rendering effect.

## 5. High-priority findings

### H1. Expanded venue geometry does not constrain comfort teleport

[10-ui.js](../js/club/10-ui.js) registers the original room walls and DJ platform as
teleport blockers, but not the new vestibule/bar/mezzanine geometry. The floor slab
continues beyond the closed entrance.

**Reproduction:** production IWER, comfort on, 1.6 m tracked eyes, stage-facing XR
origin; use ARRIVAL, aim the right controller 35 degrees down toward the street door,
hold/release the teleport stick. Camera moved from `(0,1.6,5)` to
`(-0.25,1.61,6.7422)`. The closed vestibule ends at `farZ=6`.
This is actual teleport input, not a direct write to the escaped destination.

The existing mezzanine target gap has the same registration boundary: only the main
floor is a teleport target. Update that existing backlog item to include blockers,
destination ground level and mode-swap re-registration. Test real controller arcs at
the entrance, bar and balcony rather than only at the original stage boundary.

### H2. Persistent offline asset ownership omits people and environment

[11-audio-crowd.js](../js/club/11-audio-crowd.js) loads character containers directly;
[02-lifecycle.js](../js/club/02-lifecycle.js) directly loads the warehouse environment.
Neither goes through the advertised persistent cache, while [sw.js](../sw.js)
explicitly excludes GLBs and environment binaries.

| Warm visit / offline revisit | Online | Offline, HTTP cache cleared |
|---|---:|---:|
| App `ready` | true | true |
| NPC count | 10 | 0 |
| Local rig present | yes | no |
| Environment texture ready | yes | no |
| Cached surface sets | 6 | 6 |

The probe retained IndexedDB and Cache Storage. Their keys confirmed four equipment
models and 21 surface/speaker textures, but no character/environment entry. A normal
HTTP-cache hit can hide the problem. Use the existing cache and body-timeout helpers,
not a second SW binary cache. Also cover disposal and slow-body handling in that path.

### H3. Headset delivery is still an evidence gap, not a passed budget

The balanced resource snapshot passes, but 4x XR pipeline MSAA, 1.2 framebuffer scale,
transparent effects, skinned people and recently added venue assets remain unmeasured
on the target Quest 3S. No new FPS claim is justified. Update the existing hardware
baseline task to include the bar, bins and balcony; retain asset-specific acceptance
checks until a sustained headset capture exists.

### Existing high-impact realism work, still open

- **People:** fixed clips/social roles have improved variety, but there is no evolving
  social-state/gaze/navigation scheduler. Do not describe the current room as containing
  only synchronized dancer clones.
- **Grounding/light coherence:** strongest-head slot assignment and NPC contact shadows
  now exist. Equipment grounding and true occlusion of moving lights remain incomplete.
- **Close interaction:** the local body exists, but hands/venue props have no physical
  contact response. Improve a small number of meaningful contacts, not general physics
  by default.
- **Asset clearance:** resolve the remaining Mixamo question before public release.

## 8. Immersion assessment

Strengths: readable stage focus, spatial PA direction, authored musical tension,
nearby people, local embodiment and differentiated perimeter destinations.
Breakers: escaping the shell, offline depopulation, seated remote floor penetration,
dead contact surfaces, repetitive human behaviour and the large permanent credit plate.
At 739x553 the measured plate occupied **19.2%** of the viewport; at 375x667 it occupied
**26.1%** and blocked input. This is more than a subjective dislike of an overlay.

No evidence supports adding toilets, additional rooms, ordering systems or complex
crowd AI as immediate requirements. They are product choices after core delivery works.

## 9. VR/WebXR assessment

Architecture is purposeful: one gated entry button, optional capability detection,
separate XR post-processing, teleport/snap-turn comfort default, safe-mode choice
before rendering and no artificial VR head bob. Body IK and controller rays exist.
The frame-bound preset probe found no independent balcony-to-XR calibration defect;
that candidate was not added to the backlog.

Risks are specific: expanded-venue teleport coverage, inactive-hand first clicks,
untested comfort-off stairs, synthetic-only hand-roll evidence, unknown stereo
transparency comfort and unmeasured hardware budgets. The emulator uses patched
reference-space offsets and a mono image; neither validates physical latency or comfort.

## 10. Desktop assessment

Desktop has a real control surface, travel presets, keyboard navigation and meaningful
quality tiers rather than being only an XR debugger. Online startup and the constructed
venue are testable. Remaining issues are narrow-layout input obstruction, offline
degradation, post-XR SSAO/environment drift and the known final brightness gain.
No cross-browser or gamepad parity claim was established.

## 11. Lighting and atmosphere

The show director owns cue decisions while the VJ director continues beat and palette
work. Master dimming, moving-head flash-rate governance and continuous sheet phase
have current regression coverage; the prior review's fixed defects were not reopened.
Safe Mode gates strobe paths and reduces the warehouse wall's impulse.

Keep real lights enabled and dim them rather than causing material-slot churn.
Keep the two/three strongest surface-light slots and scoped fixture accents until a
measured alternative exists. NPC contact decals are useful grounding but not cast
shadows from equipment or people. Beam geometry and room bounce remain approximations;
do not label them full volumetric transport or global illumination.

## 12. Materials and rendering

Positive: shared factory materials, imported light budgets, forced opaque character/
equipment surfaces, single-mesh LED wall, local ORM/normal maps, dim warehouse environment
and conservative XR effects. Desktop SSR and motion blur are not meant to run in XR.

Open: half-gain desktop output, restoration drift, opaque glass appearance, static-probe
limitations and no dynamic shadow generator. The actual environment texture's readiness
matters: a non-null texture reference alone is insufficient, as the offline probe shows.

## 13. Crowd and character realism

Balanced has six dancers, two social guests, a DJ and bartender in the measured scene.
The ten NPCs each have a contact shadow; the local player is additional embodiment.
Shared materials and at most six meshes per NPC are good asset constraints.
Upper-tier guests, independent phases and non-dance clips improve distribution.

The remaining problems are behavioural continuity, animation variety, gaze, personal
response and mixed character style, not simply headcount. A low-frequency role/state
scheduler using existing skeletons is preferable to adding more people. Remote rigs
are capped at four; additional people remain primitives. Ground/eye separation must
be resolved before marketing seated multiplayer embodiment as correct.

## 14. Spatial audio and music

The real audio graph correctly puts PA energy on the visible side in the executed
desktop and XR probes. For example desktop x=+4 facing the stage measured left/right
RMS **0.1583/0.1444**; x=-4 measured **0.1416/0.1602**. This proves directional mapping,
not acoustic realism, loudness safety or listener preference.

Music and ambience have separate user controls. Entry music is disclosed/optional,
the latest-episode queue has tests, and custom source selection exits that queue.
Room reverb, sub response and occlusion are authored distance/position approximations,
not solved from the bar, deck and doorway geometry. The analyser's 256-point FFT and
0-11 "bass" bins include much more than sub/kick energy; retain the existing calibration
task rather than changing bins without retuning the show.

No live podcast, headphones audition, hearing-safety assessment, wall-occlusion audio
measurement or two-peer voice session was performed.

## 15. Physics and interaction

The chosen interaction budget is reasonable for a standalone headset: collision
geometry, rays, menus, presets, opt-in haptics and body IK. It does not promise real
glass, drink pouring or movable heavy speakers. Prioritize solid navigation boundaries
and one or two lightweight contact responses at rails/booth controls.
Desktop stair collision validation does not establish XR stair gravity behaviour.

## 17. Optimization opportunities

1. Measure worst-case Quest cues and the expanded route before reducing detail.
2. Re-profile mirror raycasts; then bound candidates or use room-plane intersections
   where equivalent, preserving geometry occlusion and visual placement.
3. Batch repeated mirror rays/spots without losing tier density or cleanup ownership.
4. Reduce skeletal evaluation only with measured animation LOD and a repetition review.
5. Preserve the single LED mesh, merged bottles/steelwork and shared stool resources.
6. Evaluate a smaller Babylon bundle only against parse/startup evidence, not its raw
   byte count alone.

These correspond to existing backlog roots; no additional speculative performance
issue was created. Offline byte reuse improves reliability without reducing visual quality.

## 18. Architecture and refactoring

The main risk is cross-surface ownership, not missing abstractions: all binary loads
must share persistence/deadline rules, all walkable geometry must participate in the
navigation policy, and ground height must have a consistent meaning across local and
remote people. The newly found defects occur precisely at those boundaries.

Keep the load-order contract and named orchestration phases. Do not introduce a
parallel module list, ECS rewrite, universal physics engine or renderer migration
without a measured requirement. Private Babylon internals used by diagnostics/exposure
and the deep inheritance chain justify focused upgrade tests, not cosmetic churn.

## 19. Cleanup and dead assets

The build derives script order and allow-lists model references rather than shipping
the whole source model tree. The asset manifest and optimization contracts provide
useful protection. No binary was proved safe to delete in this pass and none was deleted.
The script-oriented code retains disabled modular branches and obsolete comments;
these are lower-value cleanup than the reproduced loading/navigation defects.

Historical backlog prose must not be mistaken for current facts: PA attribution,
NPC contact shadows, local embodiment and root dependency health have all changed.
The relevant current-state notes were corrected without erasing unresolved history.

## 20. Testing

Strengths: 171 real/mocked Node contracts, actual GLB rig tests, body/musical timing
thresholds, lighting exclusivity/safety checks, optimization budgets and production
browser tests. The rig suite is not merely a mock that assumes a planted foot.

Gaps exposed by this review:

- Whole-visit offline restoration with ordinary HTTP cache evicted.
- Controller teleport through new venue boundaries, including after comfort swaps.
- Seated remote ground/head separation rather than only the 1.7 m standing convention.
- Responsive hit testing with credits present; a visible button is not necessarily clickable.

## 21. Accessibility and comfort

Pre-entry photosensitivity warning and Safe Mode, opt-in haptics, teleport/snap turn,
labelled controls and keyboard focus protections are significant strengths. Safe Mode
is intentionally off by default; the review's manual visual work explicitly enabled it.
These controls and rate tests are not a photosafety certification.

The responsive credits obstruction is an actual accessibility/usability defect.
Preserve attribution and contrast while making controls independently reachable.
Seated remote grounding, inactive-hand selection and comfort-off stairs need explicit
regressions. A screen-reader and in-headset seated/standing audit remain outstanding.

## 22. Compatibility, privacy and deployment

Verified locally: Chromium production desktop and IWER behaviour, root build/static
checks, root/worker dependency audits. CI defines Windows/Linux and Node 20/24 checks;
this review did not run that remote matrix or verify the live Pages setting/deploy.

WebXR is a secure-context API. Do not treat the README's plain private-LAN HTTP example
as proof of an ordinary Quest setup: use HTTPS or device-localhost forwarding and
verify `isSecureContext`/XR support on the device. This review did not test Quest's
developer overrides. References:
[MDN WebXR](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebXR_Device_API),
[WebXR requirements](https://developers.google.com/ar/develop/webxr/requirements).

Security/privacy boundaries inspected include same-origin core scripts, restricted DOM
sinks, safe media URLs, opt-in sockets/mic and explicit shared-music listening consent.
The Node suite exercises relay protocol limits; no independent penetration test or
live relay verification was done. The worker tooling advisories are not proof that the
deployed relay is exploitable. Root audit success does not cover its separate lockfile.
Header-based framing policy is host-dependent; GitHub Pages does not apply `_headers`.

the inspected Chromium views. Suitable for a controlled online demo with known caveats.
Blockers/prerequisites: asset clearance, persistent offline promise and reachable controls.
Top realism/immersion issues: character behaviour, contact/lighting grounding, UI obstruction.
Top performance issue: no current representative hardware timing, not a proved FPS failure.

### VR / WebXR: Not ready

**Confidence:** High that sign-off is premature; not a claim that Quest necessarily runs poorly.
Blockers: reproducible comfort-teleport enclosure escape, unresolved release provenance
and absent real-headset frame-pacing/comfort evidence.
Top realism/immersion issues: physical boundaries, seated remote bodies, hand/contact response.
Top performance risks: stereo/MSAA/transparency/skeleton costs and thermal behaviour remain
unmeasured. The top five next actions are ranked in section 23.

## Reproduction notes

- **Offline:** fresh isolated browser profile, entry music off, balanced/Safe Mode on;
  enter and await model completion plus SW control. Record NPC/rig/environment readiness
  and IndexedDB keys. Use CDP `Network.clearBrowserCache`, preserve origin stores, set
  the context offline, reload and enter. Assert the before/after counts in section 5.
- **Teleport:** use the repository IWER reference-space shim, stage-facing yaw before
  XR entry and comfort on. Call ARRIVAL from an XR-frame callback (as the in-world menu
  does). Aim right controller toward world +z, down 35 degrees; drive IWER `thumbstick`
  `(0,-1)` for 12 frames, then `(0,0)` for 12. Check camera z against
  [VenueLayout](../js/venueDressing.js)'s closed `farZ`.
- **Credits:** at 375x667 inspect the two rectangles, call `elementFromPoint()` at the
  camera toggle centre and attempt an ordinary Playwright locator click. Do not force it.
- **Remote rig:** after real crowd containers load, create an isolated `AvatarManager`,
  call `updatePeerState` with eye y=1, then `update(1/60)`; inspect root and pose heights,
  and dispose the manager. No socket, mic or external guest is involved.
