# ADE / Remote Floor

## Independent discussion draft

Prepared 18 October 2026. No ADE affiliation, artist participation, venue agreement,
music clearance or broadcast capability is claimed. ADE is referenced to identify
the intended pitch recipient. The owner supplied the official ADE26 brandpack;
this concept uses its anniversary mark without claiming an
approved collaboration. Public use still requires permission and brand guidance.

## One set. Two dance floors.

Some electronic-music fans cannot make it to Amsterdam or enter a physical club:
health, disability, caring responsibilities, distance and cost can all be barriers.
Offer them another way to share a selected set with friends, in a browser or headset,
while seeing the real performer and hearing the same performance.

This is an additional audience experience, not a replacement for a physical visit
and not a claim that a headset recreates the social or sensory experience of a club.
Desktop access, adjustable sound, seated use and photosensitivity controls matter
as much as the avatar.

## Proposed first collaboration

One willing venue, one consenting artist, one cleared set, a small invited audience
and an accessibility co-design group. Start with the real DJ's live camera image
inside the virtual booth and the venue mixer's stereo audio feed.

In parallel, rehearse a tracked 3D likeness with the artist off-stage. Graduate to
a live avatar only if the capture is comfortable, recognisable and synchronised.
The initial show must still work if tracking is unavailable.

The virtual room is currently NOCTURNE, not a digital twin of the source venue.
Venue-specific architecture, official ADE dressing and artist likenesses would be
separate approved creative work. A single broadcast can feed multiple small social
rooms; even 32-person social rooms are not a festival-scale broadcast network.

## What ADE and a venue could gain

- An experiment in access for people not reached by physical attendance.
- An artist-approved digital presence rather than an unrelated generic DJ.
- A new way to gather friends around a shared set, on desktop as well as in VR.
- Potential ticket revenue: organisers could sell access to the VR part alone,
  or offer a combined ticket for the whole physical-and-digital experience.
- A measurable pilot before any commitment to a festival-wide product.

Reach, incremental revenue and partner interest are hypotheses to test, not proven
benefits. No ticket price or revenue split is assumed.

## Ticketing and small social rooms

Organisers could choose VR-only tickets or a whole-experience ticket bundling
physical attendance with digital access. Desktop access should remain a
first-class option in the digital offer. Ticket scope, rights, pricing, refunds
and revenue sharing would be agreed with the participating partners; event
ticketing is a proposal, not an existing capability of this demo.

ADE or the venue could set up multiple social rooms for the same performance,
each capped at an agreed number of networked guests. Friends could attend
together without putting the entire audience into one room. This branch combines
QuestDev's scaling experiment with QuestProtect's safety controls. The shared
production relay admits up to 32 connected guests in every room following the
coordinated frontend/database/relay rollout on 19 October 2026.
A pilot can use a lower cap validated on the target devices. Total ticket capacity must also
respect tested media-delivery capacity and available moderation.

All rooms would receive the same authorised performance through a separate
broadcast service. Event-room setup, ticket checks, room allocation and
cross-room playback synchronisation still need to be built and tested.

## Safety for an official event

The integrated controls include first-use online safety guidance, two-way
Report & block, host-only report notifications and existing kick, ban and lock
tools. Selected-recipient chat and microphone audiences are restricted to private
rooms; public-room audiences remain room-wide, subject to personal mutes and blocks.
These controls do not make a room an official ADE space or replace trusted hosts,
staffed moderation, independent escalation or event-ticket checks. Those are pilot
requirements. The 32-person limit is not yet physical-headset performance evidence.

## What the concept demonstrates now

- A navigable club, existing audio-reactive show and fictional procedural DJ.
- Supplied ADE26 anniversary marks on yellow wall signs, entrance plaques,
  hanging flags and the DJ-table fascia, leaving the LED wall clear.
- A closable independent-pitch information panel.
- Desktop and VR exploration of the same venue; no video screen or local video player.

It does **not** stream a physical venue, capture a real artist, align motion to live
music, clear any rights, or prove physical-headset performance.
Do not present it as a live demonstration. The owner requested a public,
non-indexed review site at `https://nocturne-ade.mitwee.nl/`; non-indexing is
not access control or an ADE approval.

## The ask

An exploratory meeting with ADE's appropriate partnerships/programme team, an
interested venue, an artist representative and accessibility advisers to:

1. Select a feasible, artist-led use case and intended audience.
2. Confirm the permission route for identity, branding, music and territories.
3. Agree a rehearsal, equipment access, pilot owner and capped evaluation budget.
4. Explore VR-only versus whole-experience tickets, room caps and moderation.
5. Define success and a go/no-go before any public announcement.

Suggested pilot evaluation: delivery reliability, measured audio/video/motion
alignment, desktop and physical-headset usability, artist comfort, audience sense
of presence, accessibility feedback and a documented rights process.

See [the feasibility and pilot plan](ADE_LIVE_EVENT_FEASIBILITY.md) for the technical
options, equipment, working budget assumptions, risk register and source links.
