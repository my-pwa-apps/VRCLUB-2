# NOCTURNE — principal experience review

Date: 2026-10-14 (later pass). Review mode: no runtime fixes.
Actionable work is in [BACKLOG.md](../BACKLOG.md). This pass does not supersede the morning
budget/XR-entry notes in that file; it re-checks the experience after those fixes.

Evidence labels: **Executed**, **Inspected**, **Prior** (earlier the same day, not rerun), **Unverified**.

## 1. Executive summary

NOCTURNE is a client-side Babylon.js 9.28 WebXR nightclub: a basement room, stair hall, bar,
balcony, street, a composed light show, spatial PA audio, and a beat-locked crowd. It is much
closer to a place than to a shader demo. It is **not** an ultrahyperrealistic club, and it should
not be described as one.

The strongest work is systemic: the show is authored on the bar grid, the kick is detected on
the audio clock, the crowd plants its feet, the DJ's reach is measured, VR comfort does not
move the camera for bass, and the light budget is treated as a hard constraint. The weakest
work is the gap between that care and what a guest can disprove in one minute: three lights
per surface, no grounding shadows, a dry matte floor, box coats at the entrance, EXIT signs
on solid walls, a body that is not the avatar other people see, and no measured Quest 3S
frame time.

Desktop is usable and, on the tests run here, stable. VR/WebXR is implemented seriously and
still **not ready** against the Quest 3S presence target, because the expensive path is
unmeasured and several comfort/lighting contradictions remain.

## 2. Repository and architecture

Babylon.js 9.28, classic scripts on `window`, WebGL and WebXR. `VRClub` is an 11-layer
inheritance chain. Materials and lights go through factories. The show director owns fixture
state when it is driving. People are tiered GLB clones plus one local `AvatarRig`. Audio is
a media element into a PA graph with HRTF, occlusion and a separate kick/rhythm tap. Multiplayer
is an opt-in Worker relay. There is collision, not a physics world. There is no general
interaction system.

Critical path: splash and safe-mode choice, venue build, crowd, model settle, then the render
loop (show, skinning, haze, listener). XR entry waits on a payment lease and model readiness.

## 5. High-priority findings

Already open, still the right list. Not duplicated:

- Quest browsers default to `ultra`, and the flat page runs desktop SSR, SSAO and motion blur until the headset session starts.
- No Quest 3S frame-time baseline. This is the release blocker for the presence claim.
- Surfaces are lit by ambient plus two (desktop) or three (Quest) photometric slots. No grounding shadows.
- Valid near-floor tracking is mostly preserved now; the collider fitter still drops samples `<= 0.3`. Headset check still open.
- Offline visits can lose the crowd and reflection environment.
- Walking collision and teleport blockers still need to match the expanded venue.
- The local body is a UE mannequin; everyone else sees a modular person.

## 6. Medium and low findings

New this pass (see the backlog for acceptance criteria):

- EXIT signs on the left wall (`z = -17.5`, under the balcony) and beside the front door do not mark an opening.
- The coat check is seven flat boxes on a rail, at eye height on the arrival path.
- The default set is 112 kbps joint-stereo while about 12 MiB of deploy budget is unused.

Still open and rechecked: desktop sharpen gain `0.5`; PA occlusion ignores height; SSAO not restored after VR; reflections drift after VR; inactive-hand trigger; image-parity flake.

## 7. Ultrahyperrealism assessment

| Layer | Judgement |
|---|---|
| Geometry | Believable shell, stair, bar and truss. Entrance garments and some joinery are boxes. |
| Scale | Door, stair, bar, stool, DJ reach and railing grip are measured. This is a strength. |
| Materials | Real PBR sets, but one tile across long walls, a dry matte floor, and no contact wear. |
| Lighting | The *cueing* is realistic. The *transport* is not: three lights, no shadows, the LED wall does not light the people in front of it. |
| Reflections | Desktop SSR on metals only, by design. No planar mirror. People are absent from any true mirror. VR has probes and a composed mirror-ball, not a reflection of the room. |
| Atmosphere | Haze is a sprite field with a phase term on desktop. Beams need it, and they have it. It is not a volume. |
| Humans | Stylised modular people with unusually good motion. Close skin, eyes and cloth will not survive a realism claim. That is acceptable if the product stays stylised and coherent. |
| Animation | Planted feet, bar-locked dances, a DJ who can reach the knobs. Better than the meshes. |
| Physics | Collision ellipsoids and a few blockers. Glasses do not move unless a scripted hand owns them. |
| Audiovisual coherence | High on the dance floor: kick, show and crowd share a grid. Weaker on the balcony and in the entrance, where the picture changes and the mix barely does. |
| Environmental detail | Queue, bouncer, ticket desk, stocked bar, cables on the bins. Undercut by the fake EXIT and the coat slabs. |

## 8. Immersion assessment

Presence is real on the dance floor when the show is running and the crowd is on the beat.
It breaks when the guest inspects: coats, signs, the missing contact darkening, the body
that is not "you", and objects that look touchable and are not. The product is honest about
the last of these in the VJ desk; it is less honest in the coat check and the EXIT signs.

Embodiment exists (rig, VR arms, seating) and is incomplete (no near interaction, mannequin
self, no haptics by product decision — do not add controller rumble to fake bass).

## 9. VR / WebXR

Session entry is gated, lease-based, and fails closed. Locomotion swaps teleport and smooth
move correctly. The camera is not gravity-driven; the surface follower owns stairs. Comfort
mode, snap/smooth turn, and a photosensitive safe mode exist. Safe mode is off by default,
which is a recorded product choice, not an accident.

Scale of the architecture is the right order. Controller interaction is a ray into the desk
and the quick menu, not hands on bottles. Headset suitability for Quest 3S is **unverified**.
IWER proves behaviour, not frame time, stereo comfort or thermal load.

## 10. Desktop

Keyboard, mouse-look, jump and seated furniture work. Eye height is locked at 1.7 m except
when sitting. The panels are plain-language and mutually exclusive. Desktop is not a debug
view. It is dimmer than the fixtures are authored for, because sharpen is a 0.5 gain, and
one VR visit can drop SSAO.

## 11. Lighting and atmosphere

NOCTURNE's looks, movements and set-pieces are the best part of the project. Strobes are
governed. Safe mode wins over the designer. Darkness is attempted (VR ambient 0.015).

The room cannot show that design fully. A bright LED wall and a hit on `spot4` only reach
a surface if the photometric copy puts them in the first slots. Contact shadows are blobs.
There is no shadow map, on purpose, after earlier generators sat outside every material's
slots. Do not add clustered lighting or shadow maps until a Quest capture says there is
frame time to spend. A cheaper win is making the existing three slots and the haze agree
with the wall, which the photometric copy already tries to do.

## 12. Materials and rendering

Factory materials, frozen shared presets, opaque VR materials, and a packed ORM floor are
the right architecture. The dance floor is specified as matte concrete. A real club floor
in this haze would have a narrow wet sheen. Raising SSR onto the whole floor would fight
the dielectric threshold and cost a stereo pass the headset does not have. Prefer a small
roughness break-up and a few contact decals over a mirror floor.

Selective glow is correctly disabled. Bloom is the only halo. That is the right VR choice.

## 13. Crowd and characters

Fourteen dancers, side guests, one mingler, a bartender, a bouncer and a queue. Nobody is
supposed to appear twice. Motion is on the beat, including a lighter rhythm state when the
kick drops. That is rare and good.

They do not negotiate space with the guest beyond a collider, and only one person works the
room. The local first-person body does not match the assigned multiplayer look. Capsules
still stand in for guests who have not loaded. Treat this as stylised crowd theatre, not
as human realism.

## 14. Spatial audio and music

The PA is mirrored into Web Audio's right-handed space, occluded in a continuous stair
curve, and thinned outdoors. The analyser hears the dry source, so the show does not go
quiet on the street. That split is correct.

The balcony is acoustically the dance floor. Crowd murmur is one point. The default set,
which every new guest hears, is the 112 kbps file. Saved-set playback is the real product;
the default set is the first impression.

There is no camera shake for bass. Keep it that way. Kick pulse already moves fixtures.

## 15. Physics and interaction

The guest can walk, sit, teleport, and operate the VJ desk and menus. They cannot pick up
a glass, open a door (the street door opens itself), or push a stool. Scripted bar service
is convincing for the NPCs and does not include the player. That is a scope choice. The
failure mode is implication: coats, bottles and EXIT signs imply use.

## 16. Performance

### Desktop

**Not measured this pass.** Tiers exist (render scale, SSR, SSAO, motion blur, crowd count).
Prior reviews did not leave a number this pass should repeat as if it were new.

### VR

**Not measured on a Quest 3S.** IWER is 1280x720 mono SwiftShader. Ultra is the Quest
default, and the flat pre-XR page runs the desktop pipelines. That is the highest
performance risk in the repo. Do not add effects until that capture exists.

## 17. Optimization opportunities

1. Do not render the club interior from the street (already open).
2. Keep the light cap. Do not "fix" realism with more lights.
3. Spend the unused deploy megabytes on the default encode, not on another texture size.
4. Coat and sign fixes are draws-neutral.
5. Avatar simplification from the morning pass needs a close VR look before anyone raises
   mesh density again.

## 18. Architecture

The inheritance chain and the load-order contract are heavy, but they are tested and
documented. A rewrite would not buy presence. The useful boundary is already there:
directors decide, the club applies, factories own materials. Keep new work inside that.

## 19. Cleanup

No new dead-asset pass this review. `podcasts.js` remains source-only. The coat boxes
and the two EXIT quads are live, not dead. Do not delete the street-door EXIT.

## 20. Testing

610 Node tests passed, including rig, show, kick and contract checks. That is a high bar
for a WebXR app. The open gaps are the flaky image parity test, the spatial-audio browser
case, and anything that requires a headset. Do not lower those thresholds to go green.

## 21. Accessibility and comfort

Photosensitivity warning and safe mode are on the splash, before strobes. Comfort
locomotion is opt-in. Text in the VR menu was enlarged on purpose. There are no captions.
Flashing content is governed (about 3 Hz) and safe mode denies the flash gate. Good.
The remaining comfort risk is the unmeasured headset, not a missing checkbox.

## 22. Compatibility

Target is Quest 3S and desktop Chromium-class browsers. Firefox, Safari and Vision Pro
are not evidenced. Controller code uses WebXR pointers and has an inactive-hand trigger
bug still open. Do not claim Quest 3S comfort or frame time.

## 23. Highest-value improvements

1. Quest 3S frame-time capture at ultra, high and balanced, including the flat-page handoff. Blocks every other graphics idea.
2. Desktop sharpen gain to 1.0, with exposure retuned so the mean stays put, and SSAO reattached after VR. Small, already specified.
3. Height-aware PA occlusion for the balcony and under the deck.
4. Remove the false EXIT signs and replace the coat slabs. Cheap arrival-path presence.
5. A/B the default set at a higher bitrate inside the 75 MB gate.

Clustered lighting, a real WC, and planar mirrors are not next. They cost more than they
will return before the five items above.

## Verdict

### Desktop

**Ready with minor improvements.**

Confidence: high that it runs and is coherent; medium on visual polish, because this pass
did not re-screenshot the floor.

Blockers for a hyperreal claim, not for shipping the current product: sharpen gain, missing
shadows, three-light cap, dry floor, entrance props.

### VR / WebXR

**Not ready** for the Quest 3S presence target.

Confidence: medium. The emulator path is tested; the headset is not. That missing
measurement is itself the blocker.

Top realism issues: light transport, shadows, entrance props, self/other body.
Top immersion issues: false EXIT signs, coat slabs, balcony that looks different and
sounds the same, non-interactive bottles.
Top performance issues: unmeasured Quest 3S, ultra default, interior still drawn from the street.

Top 5 next actions: the list in section 23.
